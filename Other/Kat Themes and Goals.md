# Motifs

1. Choosing which quests to take – The Greater Good – Kat lobbied to tackle the sewer quests first, which she saw as having bigger consequences for the well-being of Hood Station as a community. While the mega-tower does involve members of Hood Station, the issues do not seem as directly impactful to the station as a whole.
  

2. Kat heard Keena explaining to Gideon how he could improve in a social situation and talks to her about how she approached it - Family - After seeing how receptive Gideon was to Keena, Kat realized she hadn’t really figured out yet how to deal with his naivete, typically defaulting to dry responses or comments that goes over his head after being unable to get through to him the way Keena did. Kat genuinely does care for Gideon as a brother (if an annoying one) and wants to similarly be there for him, she just hasn’t quite known how.


3. Kat continually tried to reassure Mira that she’s no longer with the Saints and isn’t trying to hurt her, but it didn’t work - Outrunning The Past- When Kat fled the Saints, she wanted to leave behind the fear and pain she evoked simply by being a member. However, even just being formerly associated is enough to send people into a panic. Maybe it’s better that she keep her history to herself.


4. Kat decided that the “machine god” of the filter must be a pitiful minor being, because Silas only got a small cut on his finger from taking a coin from the offering bowl - Power - In Kat’s experience, those with real power don’t take half-measures. To hold back or show mercy would be weakness, and Morronel is not weak like this machine god must be. 


5. Kat disparaged the Machine God as a pathetic weakling and was immediately hit with a falling pipe – Hierarchy –  That pipe hurt, and Kat realized that at least in this place, the Machine God may be more powerful than she thought (below Morronel for sure, but able to meaningfully hurt her) and expressed some respect, backing down from a being she now understands is higher on the totem pole. A spray of clean water follows from the pipe, which Kat took as a sign that her deference has been accepted.


6. Kat really didn’t want to interact with the Saints the party encountered in the Sewer and hid while Max and Keena took the lead - Fear - while Kat mostly positioned her avoidance as a safety concern for the party and for Hood Station, seeing members of the Dominion Crew brought up a flood of bad memories and a fear that if she tried to stand up to them, they would just smack her down like they always used to. Hard to say if that fear was of the potential pain, not being strong enough, or of just looking weak in front of her friends…whatever it was, it overrode every other consideration.
  

7. Kat didn’t want to even be seen/recorded in Saints territory - Outrunning the Past II - Even outside of direct interaction, Kat doesn’t want to draw the attention of the Saints by appearing on video in Westreach, especially with her friends, who could be targeted for their association with her. She’s desperate to keep the darkness and violence of her upbringing from infesting the new life she’s building in Hood Station.


8. Kat tries to keep Gideon out of the “punishment room” at the orphanage and forbids him from trying any of the drugs Az offers – Innocence – as annoying as it is sometimes, Kat also wants to protect Gideon from having his childlike innocence ripped away, something no one protected her from.


9. Kat has a panic attack about having killed people –  Just Like Them – Kat’s whole reason for leaving the Saints was to avoid having to kill someone. She struggles to rationalize how killing now doesn’t make her the very thing she wanted to avoid, especially when Silas tries to make her feel better by saying it was justified. The Saints always felt their victims “deserved it” too.
  

10. Kat strongly considers killing the Saints that Kieran was with to protect his family in Westreach - Cost of Principles - when in a position where holding to her principles could harm innocents while protecting the very people who would do that harm, Kat is shaken and unsure of the right path. She was basically making the call when an alternative was presented, and while she took it, even now she isn’t sure if she trusts it.
   

11. Kat and Gideon talk about killing people on their first watch together, having both done so now - Drawing a Line – After tiptoeing the edge following the last fight, Kat worries that if she starts finding acceptable justifications for killing people, that it will get easier and easier to justify until the act no longer bothers her. 


12. Kat makes an offering of used motor oil to the Machine God – Religion - After their rocky first encounter, Kat feels an affinity towards the Machine God, like they’ve come to some kind of mutual understanding.


13. Kat and Silas have another intense conversation about her concerns with killing people – Guilt - While Silas is concerned that she’s being too harsh with herself, Kat argues she has to feel guilty about killing to keep from becoming too comfortable with it.


14. Kat helps Stonespear steal money from the ATM – Crime – While she draws a hard line on killing people, Kat doesn’t always see the harm in other crimes if a personal impact isn’t happening in front of her. Also, she just wants the bonking to stop and it seemed like the most efficient way. 


15. Kat blames the Cael Foundation for putting an ATM in such a rough area – Deflecting Blame – Kat felt guilty about robbing the ATM when she saw Orgo so distressed, but finding out he only needed money for drugs let her justify it to herself by trying to make it out like the Cael Foundation’s choice of ATM location was enabling people to buy drugs and it was a good thing she robbed it. 


16. Unwilling to kill our hostages but worried it’ll be a problem if they talk about their kidnapping/interrogation, Kat agrees to let Jack “take care of” our captured Saints  – Willful Ignorance – Kat wanted to believe Jack was offering some alternate third path, glossing over any signs to the contrary in an attempt to keep her conscience clear. 


17. Kat is distressed when Jack returns and it’s obvious that he simply killed the hostages – Culpability – Had she chosen to put any thought into it, Kat could have understood what Jack was offering and stopped him. Instead of clearing her conscience, the attempt at plausible deniability has only made her feel more responsible for the deaths of the two defenseless men, even if they were bad people. 


18. During their watch conversation, Max tells Kat that sometimes “bad” people are simply a product of their circumstances, Kat replies that it’s hard for her to understand when people don’t leave those circumstances. – Choices – Kat and Max are in agreement that it is wrong to kill people in most cases, but their reasoning isn’t entirely aligned. Kat feels Max’s statement unfairly absolves people like full-member Saints from their choices, when there are people like her who grew up similarly and chose differently.


19. Kat wants to help Brug when he’s captured by the Titan Security Forces – Culpability II – Even though the whole point of getting the Box Boys take the Pumping Station was to remove Titan without it coming back on the party, Kat still feels a little bad seeing him reap the consequences of actions he might not have taken without her influence.


20. Morrgen says Kat’s like a daughter to her and makes Kat promise to look after Gideon in her absence – Family II – Kat is really grateful to have been basically taken in by the Oubliers without really earning it. She takes her promise very seriously, and wants to show how much she cares by stepping up during this tragedy. At the same time, she’s terrified of somehow screwing it up and letting Morrgen or Gideon down.


21. Kat doesn’t intervene when Gideon starts hurling spells in his bedroom – Letting it Out – this violent expression of emotions seems like a perfectly acceptable method of handling feelings to Kat. After all, Gideon isn’t attacking a person, just his own (and Malcolm’s) stuff. Might as well just let him get it out.


22. Gideon tells Kat not to go try and defuse the bombs and she listens – Family III – after everything that's happened, and her promise to Morrgen, Kat realizes she needs to start thinking more before impulsively taking big risks. After all, she and Gideon are pretty much each other’s only remaining family in Hood Station now.


23. Kat wants to make a deal with Victor Halden for Hood Station - Naivete - Kat naively believes that Halden will give Hood Station a good deal in good faith. She knows corpos are slimy and self-serving, but she's never really thought about her favorite sports team as a corporation that would screw her over in a heartbeat. 


24. Offering to the Machine God - After a fight, Kat makes an offering to the Machine God at a large shrine and receives a blessing in return - Religion II - Kat's regard for the Machine God is growing. She appreciates that the Machine God seems both present and straightforward. Respect and honor are rewarded, disrespect is punished, but both seem measured and practical.
