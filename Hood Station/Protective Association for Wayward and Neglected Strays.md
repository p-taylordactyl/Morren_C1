# Description

The orphanage of Hood Station was functionally a slipshod home for displaced youths from any and all backgrounds. It was originally conceived by Silas as a way to keep the unruly children of Hood Station in check and as a potential source of community service. That is until he was approached by "Miss Rosie", who had scouted Silas and figured out exactly what type of man he is. Rosetta put forth to Silas the idea of using the orphanage as a recruitment pool for an underground information network of spies that could skirt the laws and its punishments, with Silas acting as the "King" with his brilliant mind and the already established admiration of the children.

Silas was eventually swayed by Rosetta's proposition and helped build the Protective Association for Wayward and Neglected Strays. To accommodate Silas and his need to constantly leave the station on his own business, the Association needed a certain type of structure so it could run without his constant supervision, and thus a need for internal ranks and dual leadership was established-- with "Miss Rosie" as the figurehead and "Queen" of the Association. The children were given guidance and purpose by Silas and Rosetta, and in exchange they were given food, housing, education, and protection in the community. 

As they grow older and more experienced, the children are to ascend through the ranks until they eventually become legal adults and are "promoted" out of the Association and sent to the Troupe-- another loose organization led by an acquaintance of "Miss Rosie" where they can exercise their independence alongside their Association-ingrained sense of organized anarchy-- chaotic in efforts, but united in purpose.


**What they do for Hood Station**: The children of the Association serve as messengers and delivery boys for the underground community where communication devices are either ineffective or too expensive to own for the average citizen. Despite their rambunctious nature, the children are diligent in carrying out their duties for the only place they can call home.

**What Hood Station does for them**: The community of Hood Station acts as a large join parentage, taking care of the children when needed and protecting them from the law or others that would cause them undue harm. Many citizens of Hood Station come from poverty and hardship-- with some of them having lost children of their own-- who serve as guides, mentors, and sometimes adoptive families of the orphans.

**Purpose**: The underlying purpose of the Association is to serve as an underground network of child spies-- eyes and ears throughout the city-- who can gather and report important information to the leader of the association for the betterment of the community. They are able to use their status as minors to avoid the harsher punishments of the law and to weaponize public sentiments of "brutality" against children by the police and corporations.


### Leadership

[[Rosetta Thorne|Rosetta "The Thorn" Thorne]], Queen of the Association - Rosetta Thorne, or "Miss Rosie" as she's known by the community, appears as a sweet old lady-- a human in her later years of life. She has poor posture, constantly hunched over from her age, with a frail looking frame constantly draped in dresses, knitted cardigans, and gloves.
Behind the closed doors of the Association however, "Miss Rosie" discards her public facade in favor of her real self: the scorned ex-rising star of [[Bellanova Family]] who was abandoned as a scapegoat by [[Prospera Bellanova]]-- the eldest child of a high ranking Family member-- when they botched a job nearly a lifetime ago. Rosetta has a lithe and muscular build, a stern gaze, and is nearly covered in intimidating tattoos from the neck down. She has been seething and plotting her revenge against the Bellanova Family for decades-- specifically the still frustratingly young and beautiful elf who framed her. 
Rosetta "the Thorn" is nothing short of a full blown psychopath. Cold, calculating, entirely devoid of empathy or remorse, and intends to bring Prospera and her entire wretched family to a slow, shameful, and agonizing end that only someone as wizened and poetic as Rosetta could achieve.
