**Backstory**
```
Katriel was raised by her widowed father, Hadrian Buchanon in a militant religious sect of the West Valence Saints known as the Dominion Crew, who practiced a form of the Doctrine of Ordained Authority. They controlled only a handful of buildings, which were ruled with an iron fist, and more violence than was common with other crews. Kat's father embedded the tenets of the Dominion into her from a young age:

1. Law is power, and power is law
2. Respect is obedience
3. Weakness earns suffering

These lessons where beaten into her on more than one occasion, in addition to a system of rules and regulations that governed her interactions with gang members, tenants, and outsiders. Chief among these rules was to respect the hierarchy, starting with her father and going all the way up the chain to the oligarchs. Obey, or seize enough power to become the new law.

As she grew up, she started to notice inconsistencies in Hadrian and the other Dominion members' practice of the tenets, but pointing it out only ever earned her a fistfight (beating) from her imposing father, reinforcing her own weakness. More and more, she began to catch herself empathizing with the residents on her block, and found herself privately questioning if their treatment at the hands of the Saints was truly deserved. 

Kat fled Westreach at age 20, having failed her initiation ritual to become a ranking member of the Dominion by refusing to kill a man who wouldn't work for the gang. She narrowly made it into the Cloud District, but busted up her bike in the process, by happenstance just outside Hood Station. 

At the community center, the Oublier family welcomed Kat to Hood Station with open arms, helping her get acquainted with the station and adjust to life outside the strict hierarchy of the Dominion Saints. Though she feels like she has found a beautiful pocket of good in the hellscape, Kat can't help but wonder when the other shoe is going to drop. 
```

**Endline Garage**

**Origin** 
```
Endline Garage started (and remains at its core) the workshop of Thorbin Sprake, a mechanical genius and Hood Station's resident tall dwarf. After he quite literally fell into the station horribly, he set up a workshop space - at first just to build himself some prosthetics, but ultimately using it as a home base for ongoing maintenance work around the station. As the station started to grow, the garage grew with it, and eventually Thorbin expanded his projects, creating a community auto-share as well as an apprenticeship program. In his free time, he works on inventions to improve the lives of the station and its residents.

```

**What they do for Hood Station**
```
The shop builds and maintains a small fleet of community scrap autos, providing Hood Stations' residents with reliable transport, as well as offering build and repair services for residents on a pay/barter-what-you-can basis. Thorbin also offers shop apprenticeships for the unemployed, where he teaches the skills needed to keep the lights on and the engines running in the community.
``` 

**What Hood Station does for them**
```
Provides a safe haven for invention as well as a steady supply of bartering customers. 
```

**Leadership** 
```
Thorbin Sprake was a mechanic for the Titan Corporation, until he lost his legs and most of his right arm in a freak accident (surprisingly) unrelated to the workplace. The long tale he spins for curious gawkers includes but is not limited to: a carjacking, a fall down several flights of stairs, a butcher shop, a cat in heat, a dumpster fire, and ultimately, a lucky bounce onto a broken manhole cover, where he fell directly into the arms of one Memaw Shoe. Through sheer determination, she and the few founding residents of Hood Station patched the dwarf up as best they could, and he miraculously survived, which still left him with only one good arm. With the Titan Corporation uninterested in continuing his employment, NAME instead dedicated his mechanic skills to restoring both his missing limbs and the home of the woman that saved him (starting with the manhole covers). Now, Thorbin stands 6ft tall on his homemade prosthetic legs and proudly runs the Endline Garage, a mechanic (and general tinkering) shop where he builds and maintains scrap vehicles as well as working on various mechanical projects for the burgeoning station community.
```
